### Pre-meeting with BIOFUND

biofund would fund the project

45 staff, mostly rangers. no ecological staff, community development staff. biofund considers the staff low. They did the MET score.

He thinks the area is going to be poorly managed once the technical staff is gone. WWF had a lot of staff. Not great, and needs improvement but it's a starting point.
Mixed patrols are done in some places
Two boats, medium size
6 ranger stations
revenues are centralized but there are ways (difficult) to get around that and manage the funds
7% of revenues go to anac, 20% of the remaining goes to communities, the remaining is for the lodge
types of co-management,

- technical partnerships: pay for some technical areas. they would report to ANAC
- full co-management: two partners have equal say (Bazaruto). Partners have the final say.
- and a third type... that they don't know how to explain. Its SPV where everything goes in it.

### First Island

Two twin-engine South African boats plus two open fiber boats with 40hp and 70 hp. the big boat is brand new (two 150hp), the second twin engine has new engines (two 115hp). boat is not new but good visiting. they bought them last year (6+ months ago?) but have basically no usage. they have the instrument but they don't use it at all.

they have a communityamaged area, no take zones in the park.

all islands are ntzs in the proposed management plan. not sure about the current one.

they also have 12 people that have been trained as rangers but are paid by the project.

consultation process for the new Integrated management plan has just started. it needs to be approved by multiple minuster but it's a 10 years process. in the meantime,there is the old GMP, and there will be a preliminary plan that is approved by the minister and can be used prior to the full plan.

for patrols,they use a smaller boat with 40hp. rangers are taking selfies on the boat.its the first time they do it. captain has done it a lot tho.

they have fixed and temporary tented camps along th coast. Nothing on the islands. each camp with 5/6 rangers on rotation.

they have 3 boats. two large and one with one 40hp. they would prefer larger boats. The big boat does about 50l per hour at 45km/h

they have earth ranger with control center but problems with electricity. they have not done any patrols in recent time because of lack of funding. they have a drone they are trained to use. those are the only patrols they do ATM.

there are a few lodges on the coast. some tourists come but no entry fee is paid.

they dont record infractions because (they say) they dont have a lawyer

they are building a new coordination center, which is where they want to be based. the other center in town will remain for admin.

they want to move the ops center from town to coordination center (4km away)

### Mafamodo (2nd island)

there is a ton of beach seines. on the islands in from of Angoche. It appears to be done on the side of the channel, which is mostly sand. it's also quite deep. not sure the seines drag but at the market I have seen a.few species of fish that you would.normally find at the bottom (like sole).

the island has an observation tower and a couple of rooms, which could easily host a couple of rangers. also, it hosts a population of seabirds (gavincha de dorso petro), which nest there (not this season). lots of rats on the island and fishers. not sure how the birds are doing. Wondering if

it seems that the reef side of the islands is not fished but I could not get in the water.

the team is very keen on the protection of two forests. they do land patrols and list illegal logging as a major issue (second after illegal gears).

they store fuel under the wood. isn't this an issue?

not sure if their facilities are equipped for marine uses. they don't have boat access in Angoche, no diving gear/storage or compressor. don't know if they can dive or do ecological surveys. probably not. they lack that capacity completely. they get surveys done through universities.

no data collectors at landing site. we should get someone to monitor sharks right away. I have not seen any (not rays), which is a bit surprising.

### Third Island

The third island, quite a bit more reef fishing. Signs of fish skins on the beach. even octopus and sea turtles. not sure why this island only... maybe it's more accessible? it looked very easy to get to the reef here, but maybe it was the tide or the better weather.

the island has quite a bit of unfinished development. The developer, south African, is finishing the work on the forth island (caldera ). it was slowed by cyclone and COVID. lots of the structure is broken now. They started before APAIPS was gazetted so now they will have to renegotiate the terms of the lease. ANAC likes the idea of hosting rangers on the island. They would have to negotiate with the developer to have some space.

## Financing

ANAC does not offer tourist services for MPAs, but they would like to. They do for terrestrial parks with safari cars, but not for marine PAs. This is something that WCS could help them with, if needed. But presumably the developer will bring that service?

ANAC could be doing something like MPRU does in TZ in DMRS where they manage a small restaurant on the beach. They could give a concession to a local provider. This for th first island next to Angoche.

in terms of financing, Biofund has a 3mil grant, plus a perpetual grant that they want to give to the xomanager for something between 50 and 250k per year to cover operations in the MPA. Most staff is paid by ANAC already so this would be to cover ops only. BUT, ops are likely to be somewhat expensive. The teams spend long time in the field and they need food and supplies and possibly cash. They also requested internet and better services. Also, the island are not far but the boats are quit big. They need posts on the islands and smaller boats for patrols on the reef.

The big initial investment will be the management planning process. There are quite a few fishers and stakeholders. It's also multi government and needs plenty of support

The zoning will need to be negotiayed with the communinities and will likely need compensatory measures. Quit e a few fishers fish on the reefs and with beach seines.

Also quite a bit of industrial vessels on the channel. Presumably they would like some agreement on the navigation pathways. If it can be agreed with fishers (and demarcated), then we could ask them to pay a concession fee.

### Forth Island (caldera )

MUCH more developed. Also better design wiyb less concrete.it looks pretty much ready to go. What about waste management here? Here ANAC has a post. with tents. it was agreed with the developer since they guard the materials on site.

Met Jack the developer. He invested 40mil with banks to develop. It will be the first international resort in Moz. They will fly ppl with helicopter from Nampula and Nacala. It takes less than two hours. They are willing to support even with fuel, lodging, and maintainamce for the boats. They want ANAC to do patrols. They will open. in 2027 if they secure loan from.bank. ATM they are 70% done. They have permanent presence and ANAC is welcome. He is upset with Chinese mining development. It's much worse than the others. He sounds like the concession agreement is signed and secured. If ANAC wants to negotiate terms, he will be a bit defensive. He needs to see enforcement effort from the MPA. He agrees that demarcation would be essential. He welcomes ecological survey and has a compressor. Tanks need maintenance.

I went snorkeling in the channel side and it's clearly overfished. I saw a school of jacks which could skew the survey but generally very low biomass. No trigger fish and no parrot fish. Few small sweet lips. Mostly angel fish and damselfish. all running away so high pressure. Coral is also in bad condition but looks like old dead rocks. Recent branching corals dead. large massive polities survived and some encrusting coral alive. some signs of recovery. I did not check the drop offs but possible that they get cool water from the channel. Might be an avoidance area. Also, I heard dolphins and whales in the water. and Jack reports a lot of turtles. also hatching in th water, usually around December.

The Australian mining company KELMAR is quite good. They have a community person, a botanist and an enforcement team to help protect and r store the local forest of endemic trees. They have a fishing exclusion area by the loading pier.Nit big and not demarcated.

The mining areas are mapped and there is a link for online database to see where they are and in what state they are. The mining happens inland but it overlaps with that park. There are no obvious issues related to pollution,other than the extensive marine traffic.

Generally,it looks like everything just started now in APAIPS. Everything is new in terms of equipment, infrastructure and even the team at ANAC.

ANAC is saying that they cannot do much about the mining companies because they are under another ministry. They the MPA should not include them. Their reputation is compromised because of that, where stakeholders complain with them about the mines.

Along the southern coast, there is Beach seines all along the dune. To me, they could propose stopping beach seins around all the islands, whitest keeping it, related along the coats. I would conduct a stock study and seasonally study. it's probably good to have a closed season. the pressure is very high. I would also want to check the substrate. Probably a good idea to do a BES around the area. Also marine mammals transectes and beach data recorders. This all area would be very good for turtles and rays.

I think a lot of this fish does not get to the landing site at all. It's likely possible to move I from the beach directly. Also, there are a few sites with fishing camps along the coast, where fish is likely collates and shipped.

In Moma, they have a new Chinese mining project that is not operational yet.

the islands in the south are quite far. there is a site in pebani but far from beach. to come to Fogo in the south, they go from MoMA (two hours at least to get to fogo) via the post on larde (between Angoche e moma).

we should do surveys with the drone for marine mammals and sea turtles.also would be good to use it for mines.they are hard to reach by car.

it would be good to visit the camps from ANAC. the team was not happy with the conditions it seems.

The only tented camp operational is in Plebani, bit ita hard to reach it by car because of the rivers.

Fogo is far from Moma and the coast. There is no phone reception. The sea is much rougher. You need good conditions to get up here.

The advise would be to have a ranger station in caseorina, keep a 40hp there, then keep a boat nearby (Pebani), not Moma (too far). You can move the car back and forth from Angoche.

Caseorina and epidendrum would be good for a ranger post in this area. Moma is too far for patrols.

Do the rangers have swimming and boat training?

### Islas the Fogo (5th)

not much fishing it seems. it's far. Need to check the reef. It has a light house, don't know who manages. it has new solar panels and water system. The tower could be renovated and used as watch tower. it's not usable ATM.

Nice ecolodge. They are not operational anymore but it's more advanced. they got the number of the HR person. the owner has not been around for a while. he is American and lives abroad.

The reef is way better than the other island with the lodge (caldera). It has largish (50cm to 1m) branching corals and encrusting corals in shallow waters. water is noticably colder than caldera. Coral cover is also.higher (20% or more). I only checked the leeward side, same as caldera.

Met fishers that speak Swahili. they lived years in kilwa and mtwara. they say no problem in quirimbas and that there is more fish in Mozambique. They are based off Moma and they have a fish processing facility (d ying raks) for reef fish. Hook and line and small jarife nets. I did a bit of snorkeling also in the flat in the northern side. Also some coral, better visibility, and some fish. Fishing generally low here as well.

It's possible that this group of islands in the south is generally more resilient than the north, because it's more offshore. This would be an avoidance strategy.

heard whales again, but only in the leeward reef. I wonder if maybe they are in the channel?

I need to get the baseline ecological surveys conducted by WWF years back and the old GMP. Also, plot the data in. mermaid.

For the GMP, one can do an amendment of the existing old one and use that one. One can add an MSP section and new zones to have enforcement authority on the islands. This could be discussed with the communities during Y1, for example.

Rangers to 18 days field and 10 at home but they would be open to more flexible solutions here. It would make sense to have shorter missions on the posts to replace ppl and supplies. it's also unlikely they can bring fuel and food for 5/6 ppl for 18 days on the boat they have. They should keep the missions shorter and provide good accomodation.

### Islas Caseorina (6th)

the leeward site is mostly rocks. could not find much coral. the topography is a bit different with an extended shallow area all around it. I think there will be corals in the northern part and off the fringing reef but I could.not see many. The few there were massive porites. also sign of dead coral. likely old but some more recent acropora also dead. hard to say if it was broken by nets or other.

Same as Fogo, this one has a fishing camp. It looks quite established. They even have ice coolers and weight balances. They sell all the fish to a buyer. He gives them equipment and gets fish from them.

They catch lots of Petra fish. No sharks apparently. They sell it for 70MOZ per Kg to the buyer. They also seem to harvest large shells, not sure what for. The shells seem to be left around.

We did not stop at the next one because we were late, but it looks the same as caseorina. Visible fishing camp. Quite a bit of vegetation. Also shallow waters around it. Both Caseorina and the next one have rocky substrate. In the next one, there is a watch tower. Seems to be In a batter state than the one in Fogo.

### Final remarks

In the speeches with final remarks, WCS highlighted the fact that this plane has a lot of potential, that the ANAC team is clearly ready to do more, and that there is a clear strategy that can be implemented. also, it was noted that the narrative on the coastal mining needs to be reframed in the context of the MPA and the impacts on the marine ecosystems.

ANAC, pointed out that APAIPS is also it's communinities and that conservation solutions will need to take into account their needs. Also, that the mission did not include the terrestrial components (the costal.forests), which are also an important part of APAIPS.
