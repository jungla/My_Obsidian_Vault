Co-management history,

- RUMAKI Seascape 2005
- Marine Park in Mafia created strong opposition
- Somanga was the first BMU in 2009
- In 2014 and 2016, all the other CFMAs were created (SOMAKI and KIRULINA)
- All CFMAs have co-management plans, some of them expiring soon. They were developed by SeaSense, AFO, IUCN, and WWF.
- The BMUs have bylaws that they can enforce. If it's a violation of the fishery regulations, they transfer the evidence to the fishery officers.
- CFMA only does coordination. The BMU does most of the work. Including MCS. The CFMA checks whether the BMU is good at managing the finances, for example. It is also responsible for conflict resolution.

In 2010, they did a BMU self-assessment of the governance.
in 2023 the BMUs were audited
In 2025, they got support from WWF to improve accountability. There should be a standard to limit how much is spent on BMU meetings. Some CFMAs spend most of the money on meetings.
in 2025, they developed the revenue guidelines for BMUs to agree on how much BMUs should be paid and to do what.

eCAS has data for all major landing sites in Kilwa, and the MLF shared that with WWF and the BMUs.

They had a Fishers Forum. They discussed all issues related to fishery management.

There are several temporary closures in the CFMAs already, with some of them that are overlapping with the proposed boundaries for the MRs.

Little actual revenue from the closures. They found out that at a distance of 7 km from the village, the cost for patrols matches the revenues from the closure (but it will likely depend on the size and type of closure).

# Day 2

## Reef and Ruins

We went to the ruins a bit further away in Songo Mnara. The ruins are a good visit. We passed by a village, but TAWA wants to sell coconuts instead of the community.

We went snorkeling. The first site was very close to the island with poor visibility and lots of sedimentation. The reef was shallow with lots of seagrass and some bommies. Ideal site for an octopus closure. Lots of fishers in the area. They told us that there is a temporary closure nearby.

We also went to a second site further down, close to the open ocean. Much better visibility, with a very nice, extensive coral cover. Low fish density with some large fish at depth. This is their nursery site, but it's abandoned. The coral is dense with low rugosity and likely would not hold many fish.

### Way Forward

We met at the office after that and discussed a way forward. We agreed to,

- prepare a swot analysis of the MPA vs MR options
- complete a stakeholders list to prepare for consultations
- Finalize information document for each BMU
- Complete analysis on main governance differences between BMU/CFMA and VLC/CMA. Also in the context of the new SOPs for VLCs that were developed in Tanga. It's possible that we could request revision of the VLC and CMA rights so that BMU can have more freedom and retain revenues.
- The difference between MPA/MR should be communicated properly to communities

A third option between only MPA and only MR is to have an MPA in TAS4FI and/or KIRULINA, and reserves in the north. This would provide better management for the ruins and the reefs near Kilwa. The rest could be reserves and OECM. This is similar to MPRU's proposal, but with MPA boundaries that match the CMFAs.

It will be important to present the boundaries of the Marine Reserves and zones of the MPA without clear boundaries to avoid discussions during consultations. The work of determining the boundaries of the Reserves and zoning of the MPA will come later.

Fishers like the MPA option because they have seen the benefits from Mafia and want the same. They also see the issue of depleted resources and the many conflicts they have to resolve on their own. The CFMA system seems ineffective in the case of the Songo Songo and Kivinje conflict.

# Day 3

## Songo Songo Visit

takes about 40min from Kivinje
from SS, about 20 min to the sandbank near Kivinje.

The BMU talked about patrols the most. Land and sea patrols. They have several issues with nets, spears, blasting, gas, sea turtles, and sea cucumbers. Ringlets are also a problem in shallow waters. A lot of ppl doing fishing, but few ppl to do patrols.

They would like torches because fishers goal at night. For spearfishing. Little effort and big catch.

Q: where do they do patrols?
Q: do they have migrant fishers?

It's important to have a good approach. The BMU is perceived as few ppl doing the work, but everyone depends on them.

They do two or three patrols a month. Problem with budget.

They leave at 7 and back at 3pm. They do all around the islands here.

100l of patrols with a 40hp. They give them a fine and if they do it again, they are prohibited from fishing.

They can take spearguns and not monofilament.

Sources of funding come from dago fishers paying rent,  and the 2% tax, they also keep some of the infractions and get funding from the octopus closure.

How are revenues accounted for? They have 250 members; they have guidelines; every 6months they report back to the BMU. Also, after they open the octopus the meet to report back on the revenues.

They have a conflict; they have a system to resolve it.

Since they are doing patrols for a long time, how are things going? Improving or not? They have the problem that illegal fishers come from all around. Illegal fishing goes up and down, depending on how often they do that. Most fishers come from far so it's hard to educate them.

The idea is to have coordination in the CFMA. Is this working? Patrols should be in the CFMA.

The CFMA does not have the finances to support the patrols. They are just reporting on the patrols. They need to capacitate the CFMA to be able to support joint operations.

They have a fishery management plan, and from that, BMUs do their work plan. The WP is good.

### Octopus Closure

For the octopus closure, they have done it 13 times; they started in 2017. It was a very hard process. To make it work, it took time.

They went to Zanzibar to see good reefs and saw what was happening. In Kukuu Pemba in 2016, the Mwambao project, presumably.

They closed in November the first time, and it was not good. They have improved after that. They do public announcements in the village and around when they close it.

BMU presented the project to the community with a budget; they proposed this to the district; they called an assembly meeting to formalize and close.

They received support from BV, but it was small, and they don't have any more. They used to get 80% from them, but now they do it on their own. They get 20% from NGOs. They spent 20 million to manage the closure. Hamish was showing that sometimes they have a positive and sometimes a negative overall balance. Also, some issues related to buyers bribing the data collectors to declare smaller revenues.

28mil to close; they made 33.7tons equivalent to. But the money that goes to communities,

1kg is 7,500 TZS. Each kg, the fisher gets 1k TZS, and 500 TZS for the boat. The Government taxes the buyer, not the fisher.

1,300 ppl going to the ocean; 500 were women.

The closure is for all fishing techniques. When it's open, they first open for octopus only (about 3 days); on the other days, only fishing. After that, they close again. Now they only open for 5 days and then close right away.

During the opening, there are some other activities around it, like cooking and selling drinks. The whole village becomes part of this process because they get a lot of fishers from outside coming.

There should be a value chain certification so that octopus can be sold abroad.

The price for octopus is negotiated in advance, but there is no auction system for the buyers. No there is also few companies and not enough competition.

There is an ice facility. The BMU does not know about it.

They need ice because otherwise it goes bad (it did not happen often). The buyers want to buy all in one day. They can also sell the ice to other villages. The product is better if they can store it.

The buyers are ok to pay more, but only if the BMU covers the costs for transport and ice. Fishers want payment right away, not after they are paid. They cannot keep everything in cash.

WWF was here last time in 2019. They developed a value chain study.

They have problems with managing the fishers during the closure. It's a lot of fishers, and ppl sneak out without paying the BMU. Maybe MPRU could help with the management.

### Seaweed

For seaweed, there is value addition, but not much. Only one person has the processing machine. Very little value addition.

The BMU helps the seaweed farmers to connect with buyers. They connected with AFO, for example. They don't do more management.

1kg is 200 TSZ for mwani or 150TSZ. There is no fix price and competition between the sellers. For octopus, they negotiate the price.

### Relationship with the CFMA and MPA

There is little coordination between CFMA and BMUs. The BMI network is supposed to have these meetings with all BMUs, but the meetings are not happening, and the BMUs are not coordinated.

They meet in Somanga when they meet as a CFMA. Kivinje is TIM4SI, so conflicts with them are not resolved in the CFMA.

Kivinje is a problem because fishers act with impunity. They are connected with MLF, and they don't spend according to BMI guidelines; instead, they spend revenues on schools and roads.

In Mafia, they have an MPA, but they don't think they have anything to learn from it. In fact, the discussion on MPA is very delicate in Kilwa. But they recognize that they do better conservation.

It would be easier to manage illegal fishers if the government helped.

Saratoga is a fishing technique; fishers need a compressor and a generator and go down with a pipe. It's illegal, but fishers do it because they can stay for many hours underwater. They fish with kikumi. There are some sites where ppl die almost every other time they dive. The depth is almost 80 m.
