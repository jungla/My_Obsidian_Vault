NIRAS (Luca Crudeli, Elena Bellitto (NIRAS)) interviewed WCS Tanzania (Jean Mensa, Ben) for an Expertise France WIOPE mapping study, covering Pemba MPA gazettement, community governance and enforcement gaps, the stalled Kenya-Tanzania TBCA, and where small grants could add value (Ishimobahari-style no-take zones plus restoration).

### Context and Purpose

- NIRAS is mapping expertise for Expertise France under the WIOPE program across nine Western Indian Ocean countries.
- Output feeds an internal report (due 3 October) informing a technical assistance facility and possible small grants for nature-based solutions.
- Focus areas include embedded experts in regional/national institutions, transboundary conservation areas, and marine spatial planning at BBNJ.

### Pemba MPAs and Gazettement

- Northeast and Southeast Pemba MCAs were formally gazetted around the recent Our Ocean Conference.
- Same gazette also revised other MCA boundaries and formally gazetted Zanzibar's Fisheries Management Zones (no-take zones) for the first time.
- GMP process ongoing; baselines and community co-management plans done, now consolidating into a full management plan for the new MPAs.
- GMPs should embed the Local MSP process (community-scale marine spatial plans piloted by WCS, IMS and others in Zanzibar).

### Community Governance and Enforcement

- SFCs (Zanzibar) and BMUs (mainland) are structurally different but similar in practice; BMUs generally have more capacity.
- - Pemba East started from near-zero capacity as no prior marine conservation project existed there.
- Neither Zanzibar nor mainland delegates enforcement authority to communities, limiting patrol effectiveness.
- Two piloted models: communities report violations to fisheries authorities, or run joint patrols with an enforcing officer aboard (preferred in Zanzibar).
- Shift underway from SFC/BMU to CFMA/CMA governance to reduce inter-community fishing-ground conflicts; Pemba East lags Pemba West on this.

### Kenya-Tanzania TBCA

- Process has stalled: consultations went behind closed doors, borders undemarcated, NGOs and communities no longer involved.
- GIZ still runs a project but momentum is low; unclear what final instrument (e.g. biosphere reserve) would even add over existing MPAs/CMAs/CFMAs.
- Real transboundary issues are confined to ~50 km either side of the border (e.g. Kenyan fishers around Jasini); broader TBCA seen as more marketing than substance
- Core unsolved question is bilateral: how MPRU and KWS handle a fisher caught illegally across the border, not a large new governance layer.

### Institutional Reform and Grant Design

- Push to convert MPRU (mainland) and Marine Conservation Department (Zanzibar) into authorities with enforcement powers and their own bank accounts.
- Zanzibar reportedly got internal green light ~2 weeks ago; a combined fisheries and marine conservation authority could be established before year-end (not publ
- Recommended grant focus: scale the Heshimu Bahari-style model of small, well-protected no-take zones under mixed governance (govt, community, private).
- Link to coral reef restoration as an entry point for community-managed no-take zones and potential ecotourism revenue.
- Luca Crudeli pushed for more systemic, upstream integration of MPAs into wider socioeconomic value chains, including transboundary market flows.
