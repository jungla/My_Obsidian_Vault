Key Discussion Points:

YOC’s COP12 Participation and 40th-Anniversary Role
Arthur Tuda opened the meeting by framing it as a discussion of YOC’s collective participation in COP12 and its role in celebrating the Nairobi Convention’s 40th anniversary. Samantha Petersen proposed sharing both joint ideas and individual organizational plans to identify collaboration opportunities.

COP12 Structure, Scale, and Programme
Jared Bosire described COP12 as a four-day and more inclusive event than previous Convention COPs, with exhibitions, women’s and youth events, a business forum, ministerial dialogues, and potentially more than 1,000 participants. He also outlined the planned openings, the 40th-anniversary programme, and the status of ministerial attendance and side-event submissions.

YOC’s Institutional Contributions and Participation Access
Jared emphasized that YOC is entering the COP with its strongest position to date and that its regional contributions will be reflected in the UNEP Executive Director’s report. He confirmed that YOC partners would be able to participate across the COP, including high-level and heads-of-delegation sessions, while noting that organizations may need to choose between attending priority sessions and running side events.

Side-Event Decisions and Opening-Statement Process
Samantha asked when organizations would learn whether their side events had been accepted, and Jared confirmed that notifications would be sent on the 10th, with the programme expected on 20 September. The discussion also addressed preparing YOC’s opening statement, including contributions from working groups and highlighting YOC’s work during the previous year.

Agenda for Collective YOC Coordination
After Nancy Ogonje joined, Samantha restated the agenda as planning YOC’s 40th-anniversary contribution, coordinating organizational activities, and supporting the YOC opening statement. Nancy then raised the possibility of a shared YOC space for additional activities and sought clarification on participant invitations.

Shared Space, Invitations, and Registration
Arthur Tuda said the group had not yet decided whether to create a joint YOC space or additional programme, making this part of the meeting’s substantive discussion. He explained that submitted names had been forwarded to the relevant Tanzanian government office and asked members without official invitations to identify themselves so YOC could seek special requests.

Recognizing the Nairobi Convention’s Anniversary
Nancy Ogonje connected the YOC’s previous collaboration and visibility efforts with the Nairobi Convention’s 40th anniversary. She encouraged members to propose a collective contribution that would recognize the Nairobi Convention and demonstrate YOC’s role as a strong partner.

Blue Finance and Blue-Economy Contributions
Nico Willemse described ongoing communication work and proposed using the YOC Blue Finance Group’s sustainable-finance and blue-economy initiatives as part of the COP12 contribution. He suggested discussing this work during the 6–7 October focal-point meetings and linking it to the Nairobi Convention’s earlier call for sustainable financing mechanisms.

Reception, Historical Memory, and the YOC Boat
Participants considered a birthday reception with a cake, historical photographs, a slideshow, and recognition of YOC contributions. They also discussed recreating or sourcing a YOC boat as a visible photo opportunity and potentially making it a recurring regional symbol.

Ministerial Engagement and Collective Visibility
Arthur Tuda proposed three forms of participation: supporting members’ individual side events, organizing a short cocktail or formal session with ministers, and joining the larger anniversary celebration. The ministerial session could highlight YOC’s work on finance, OECMs, and other thematic priorities, while the broader event could feature the cake and boat.

Communications, Partners, and Exhibition Presence
Nancy proposed generating visibility before COP12 through organizational social-media campaigns, an updated website, and coordinated communications about YOC’s impacts. She also suggested clustered booths, recognition of members and partners, and a commemorative item for the Nairobi Convention.

Commemorative Recognition and UN Policy Constraints
The group explored possible anniversary gifts, including a customized trophy, signed display, large birthday card, ocean artwork, or a coastal-community creation. Participants noted that United Nations gift rules could restrict what the Nairobi Convention may accept, and Arthur agreed to clarify the applicable requirements.

Consolidating the Main Proposals
The discussion added a possible reflection session linking achievements from OEC to COP and regional consultations to African commitments, as well as a YOC meeting before or after COP12. Samantha summarized the leading proposals as a reception, commemorative recognition and slideshow, a YOC boat, and clustered or jointly branded booths.

Celebration Format and Objectives
The group discussed uncertainty about the format and timing of Nairobi Convention’s anniversary celebration, including the possibility of a high-level event involving the president. Participants emphasized the need for a flexible plan centered on visibility for Guy and recognition of the convention, with a gift and boat-related presentation as possible components.

Dedicated Event and Financial Support
Nancy raised concerns that the proposed slideshows and recognition activities might not fit within the main celebration agenda. The group considered holding a dedicated cocktail or separate event, while Samantha noted that member financial pledges would likely be required.

Gifts, Archival Materials, and Costing
Jean offered assistance with organizing a boat and sourcing a miniature boat or gift. She also suggested using Nairobi Convention’s historical records for a slideshow, and Samantha proposed obtaining cost estimates before seeking financial commitments.

Agreed Celebration Components
Nancy stressed that the group should first agree on the activities it wanted to deliver before developing budgets or assigning work. Samantha proposed an event showcasing member contributions, presenting recognition to Nairobi Convention, and compiling short congratulatory videos from members; the proposal received support.

Working Group and Coordination
The group discussed forming a small working group to plan the entire celebration, rather than only the video. Nancy recommended including the committee and other organizations, with the Secretariat coordinating membership and meetings because the planning window was limited.

Immediate Formation of the Working Group
Arthur encouraged participants to volunteer directly during the meeting to reduce email exchanges and save time. The immediate next step was to agree on the date for the working group’s first meeting.
