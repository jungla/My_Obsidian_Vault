> [!summary]- Flow Summary
> EJF (Will May and Beatrice) introduced the Global Toolkit project to WCS Tanzania (Jean Mensa). Co-management support looks most relevant; WCS is open to partnering.

### Project and WCS Context

- Global Toolkit is EU-funded capacity building for small-scale fishers; Tanzania phase runs October 2026 to December 2027 (14 months).
- EJF has no presence in Tanzania and is scoping key players and sites over the next 1 to 2 months.
- WCS marine program (50 to 60 staff) works in Pemba, Tanga, soon Kilwa, on fisheries management, MPAs, MCS, monitoring and restoration.

### Pillar 1: Community Surveillance (DASE app)

- Jean Mensa sees little evidence of industrial IUU in coastal waters, based on Skylight and Global Fishing Watch, though no proper study exists.
- Only caveat: a licensed national shrimp trawl fleet fishing off the Rufiji Delta (transcribed 'Rofiji'), shared with small-scale fishers.
- Inshore waters behind the baseline, which runs past Zanzibar and Pemba, are closed to industrial fishing.
- Jean Mensa advised scoping before committing to this pillar at all.

### Pillar 2: Co-management Associations

- Co-management is well established: BMUs and CFMAs on mainland, Sheia Fishery Committees and CMAs within marine conservation areas in Zanzibar.
- Organizations have converged on SMART (transcribed 'SMAT') for community MCS; WCS wants to keep building on it rather than add DASE.
- Community evidence can reach prosecutors via government officials, but few cases get there and many are dismissed.
- Opportunity: government sustainable fishing training required for artisanal licenses lacks resources and materials could be revised.

### Pillar 3: National Associations and Policy

- Candidates include a Tuna National Alliance and Tanzania Women Fish Workers Association, but Jean Mensa finds them not very active.
- Fisheries act revisions have sat in draft for years; Jean Mensa cautions against promising policy change deliverables.
- Suggested priority order: CMAs, then national associations, then DASE.

### Entry Strategy and Partners

- Zanzibar and mainland act almost as two countries with separate fisheries ministries.
  - Zanzibar ministry: open but wants involvement in implementation
  - Mainland Ministry of Livestock and Fisheries: harder to reach, lake-focused
- Formal entry via letter and concept note to the Permanent Secretary; registration, permit, or working through a partner likely needed.
- Implementing partners named: WCS, WWF, Action for the Ocean, Mombau, SeaSense, Under the Wave; TNC and FNF mostly fund others.
- Jean Mensa said WCS would be interested in a partnership, especially on co-management.

### Next Steps

- (Will May) Reflect internally on which pillars and geography fit Tanzania, then update WCS in 2 to 3 weeks
- (Will May) Follow up on the licensing training opportunity if the CMA pillar is chosen
