> [!summary]- Flow Summary
> Workshop groups presented challenges and support needs for marine nature-based solutions: restoration, governance and policy, seaweed and livelihoods, and ecotourism. Discussion covered linking restoration to tourism revenue and questioning the Tanzania-Kenya TBCA.

### Restoration Group: Challenges and Needs

- Climate impacts across coral (bleaching, mortality), seagrass (zonation shifts) and mangrove (transcribed as 'bamboo') die-off from soil changes.
  - Other issues: finance, few experts, weak enforcement and demarcation, no baselines or long-term monitoring
- Sustainability gap: communities expect time compensation, so volunteering drops when projects end.
  - Mangrove cut for fish-smoking fuel and construction; low awareness; dynamite fishing and fragment theft
- Support needed: climate-smart restoration (resilient corals), capacity building, clear exit strategies, finance, consolidated national manuals.
  - Field bases for scuba gear and compressors; demarcation boards; alternative baits; trees for fuel and building
  - Review of fines and bylaws; income-generating activities; marketing local products; stronger M\&E

### Linking Restoration to Ecotourism

- Speaker 4 proposed linking restoration sites to dive centers and mangrove tours, plus cheaper direct transplanting, to offset costs.
- Speaker 5 cited a community action plan expecting tourism near a restoration site via tour operators.
  - Speaker 4: operator income does not flow back to restoration; a return mechanism is needed

### Governance and Policy Group

- Conflicting legal frameworks between mainland Tanga and Zanzibar's Pemba seascape (biosphere reserve) and between Tanzania and Kenya; harmonization needed.
- Tanga-Kwale TBCA agreed around 2016 shows little progress in nearly 10 years; advocate government ownership and timely decisions.
- Blue carbon: unclear mandates (MPRU vs TFS), benefit sharing and carbon credit guidelines; review guidelines and harmonize mandates.
- Community management plans not linked to MPRU general plans; LMMAs (transcribed 'LLMA') need clear governance and financial sustainability plans.
  - MPRU view: integrate community priorities into the GMP, e.g. as an annex, not a separate plan

### TBCA Debate and NBS Perception

- Speaker 4 questioned whether a political TBCA is needed, since cross-border NGO collaboration already works.
  - Speaker 5: still valuable as marine life and coastal culture cross borders
- Alternative approach: national biosphere reserves per country linked by an MOU; two proposals submitted to UNESCO Man and Biosphere, awaiting response.
- Suggestions: meeting communiqués with calls to action to ministries, joint KWS and MPRU work, GIZ as a convener.
- Governments, especially Zanzibar, see NBS as backward; a planned 400 m concrete wall cost about $100,000 vs about $5,000 for the NBS option.

### Group 2: Seaweed, Livelihoods and Ecotourism

- Seaweed, beekeeping and fish value chains face post-harvest loss, market access, price swings, disease (ice-ice) and conflicts with fishers.
  - Also: finance, seedling availability in Pemba, low awareness and capacity
- Needs: training, financing deep-water seaweed farming (mostly men now), direct farmer-buyer links, marine spatial planning, product diversification.
  - A buyer works with Action for Ocean (AFO) in Tanga, sourcing cottonii (heard 'cotton leaf') from Mkinga
- Ecotourism barriers: no formalization, high entry costs, cultural fit, low capacity, sacred sites and rights, market access, few amenities.
- Core message: move community operators from informal to formal, with financing, insurance, training, community brands and study tours.
