### Annual Report: Growth and Resource Mobilization

- Membership rose from 34 to 40; six new members: Climate Gateway Africa, Sushin, MUVA, Ecotourism Kenya, ICAC, Eco Digital.
- Nine projects co-created with member consortia via six co-creation workshops and four funder-aligned webinars.
- 3.4 million in applications under review, donor responses expected by end of year; a further pipeline totaling about 6 million to be submitted soon.
- Main challenge: slow donor cycle from pre-alignment through submission, corrections and due diligence.
- African Circular reported, outside OBF, $2.6 million secured for fish waste processing in Seychelles (about 30 tons a day); another $450,000 needed to finish.

### Regional Influence and Strategy Scorecard

- Regional ocean governance strategy monitoring: 15 member initiative examples collected against a target of 10, after a July session with Nairobi Convention.
  - Plan to showcase them on OBF channels and the Nairobi Convention clearinghouse
- Events: networking session at Our Ocean Conference Mombasa (with Wetlands International, Maersk, GIZ); Nairobi Convention COP12 side event with 50+ participants.
- Legal registration as a not-for-profit company limited by guarantee was approved by ISC in March but is paused to avoid disrupting current operations.
- About 80% of partners engaged in activities; only 20% used Pangea for co-design against a 50% target.
- Budget only partially met, via GIZ, by limiting activities; business plan, hosting and handover targets fall in year three of the interim strategy.

### 2027 Priorities

- 10 priorities across five themes: foundations, growth, investment, impact, sustainability; 2027 focus is closing submitted proposals and OBF independence.
- Donor landscape shifting: NORAD largely inactive, Sweden shut five embassies; more money available via guarantees, DFI and catalytic financing.
- Aim to grow toward 50 members, adding financial institutions and private sector; activate dormant working groups and innovation clusters.
- Engagement should be member-driven, with members proposing collaboration areas rather than waiting on the secretariat.

### Swipe Invest Partnership

- Swipe Invest is the GIZ-run investment pillar of the EU-funded Sustainable Western Indian Ocean Program, launched in June at Our Ocean Conference.
- Provides financial advisory to existing, operating sustainable businesses to raise capital; not startups.
- Proposed OBF links: collective ESG advocacy, policy work on systemic investment barriers, and sharing private sector investment insights.
- Maritime skills for global employability component targets seafarer training (modern fuels, digitization, cybersecurity); ports and shipping innovation cluster proposed as the umbrella.
- Members welcomed the move from traditional science toward finance and policy; research organizations want guidance on engaging the private sector.

### ISC Governance

- ISC has 11 members plus a non-voting ex-officio; all confirmed continuing except possibly <@person3:n=Guy> from Mihari Network, who may have changed role.
- With that seat likely lost, two local or regional civil society seats remain vacant; international NGOs do not count as civil society.
- No civil society nominations came back; new members are eligible via nomination form, with accelerated onboarding and ISC decision.

### Any Other Business

- A member requested a forward plan of global events, including upcoming COPs; events will sit in a joint work plan with partner-led and open actions.
- Members urged OBF to align with DFI and project-based financing and EU Global Gateway priorities (benefits north and south).
- A 250 million euro project draws the most traction from guarantee programs, banks, DFIs and foundations; members advised to update portfolios to speak DFI language.
- Donors such as GCF are shifting to blended financing with banks as lead partners, seen as a long-term trend.
